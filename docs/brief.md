# Three Sigma Coaching: brand brief

Written by the founder, 2026-10-06. Decisions made during the blueprint are rolled up at the end.

## What it is

A five-week, in-person class at Staples Mill Library where middle and high school students learn to build working apps, websites, and games using AI coding tools.

## Who buys

Parents, likely 35 to 55 (unknown), in the Richmond area, with a child they consider gifted. They value education highly and are willing to pay for it. They are afraid their child will enter a job market where AI has made ordinary skills worthless, and that school is not preparing them for it. Some also worry AI will do their child's thinking for them. What they've already tried: unknown, possibly tutoring, enrichment programs, or coding camps.

## Who uses

Middle and high school students, roughly ages 11 to 18, in the top 5% of general problem-solving ability. They pick things up fast, solve problems creatively, and keep going when something doesn't work. Many are in honors or AP classes or have skipped a grade. They find slow-paced, repetitive classwork boring. No coding experience required.

## The promise

In five weeks your child goes from no experience to a finished, working app, website, or game they built themselves, and the ability to direct AI to build more.

## How it should feel

Authoritative, selective, warm. It must never feel like a mass-market coding camp or a sales funnel.

## Proof

- Taught by Braulio, a Virginia native and Princeton graduate (Mandarin) with over 10 years of experience teaching and coaching kids.
- The instructor is a self-taught developer with multiple apps published on the App Store, built by directing AI tools, the same method taught in the class.
- Results: none yet. First cohort. Students are pre-screened for aptitude, class capped at 10, and every family meets the instructor one-on-one before the course begins.

## The one ask

Book a free call with the instructor through the form at the bottom of the page.

## Decisions made (do not relitigate)

- **Three sigma is the outcome, not the door.** The sell is that the child ends up three sigma in AI skills for their age cohort. Top 5% is the admission screen. Copy keeps the two separate: who gets in, and where they end up. The "top 1%" headline stands.
- **Wordmark is "Three Sigma."** The business name is Three Sigma Coaching; the header and footer show "Three Sigma."
- **Yellow (#FFC845) is the colour of the ask.** Added as a second accent alongside navy and sky blue. The header button and every "Book a free call" button are yellow; yellow never appears on diagrams in the art.
- **Headlines are left-aligned and bold.** Chosen over Whoop's light weight and Headspace's centering. Warm and authoritative; never thin, never centered.
- **Typeface is Instrument Sans**, 400 and 700. Chosen on the live page against Montserrat and Epilogue; it is the narrowest of the three, so long card titles and the "1%" headline fit.
- **Section order:** hero, about the teacher, what they'll walk away with, logistics, is this right for your child, 1-on-1 tutoring, book a free call. About the teacher sits second because the founder's credentials carry the pitch.
- **No video sales letter** at the top for now.
- **Outcomes and fit traits are stacked rows with a pop-up**, Headspace-style, not cards or carousels. Pop-up colours cycle sky blue, yellow, navy.
- **Art direction is "Thread"**: flat app-brand illustration (Duolingo/Headspace feel), a navy diagram as the landscape with stylised teenagers on it. Every section hero is this style except about the teacher, which is a photograph. Model sheet lives in `src/content/graphics.ts`.
- **The form is a dummy** for now: four fields, submit appears only when all are filled, submitting does nothing.
